MIPT Critical Quantities
======================================================================

NOTE: All p_c, nu, and alpha quantities reported here are finite-size
effective estimates from L <= 24 unless explicitly labeled as literature
values. Systematic finite-size bias dominates over statistical uncertainty.

+----------------------------------------------+-------------------+---------------------------+
| Quantity                                     | This work         | Literature                |
+----------------------------------------------+-------------------+---------------------------+
| p_cross_eff (crossing of S curves, Fig 1)    | 0.203 +/- 0.017   | ~0.16  (Li+ 2019)         |
| p_fss_eff (FSS collapse, Fig 3)              | 0.209 +/- 0.008   | ~0.16  (Li+ 2019)         |
| nu_eff (correlation exponent, Fig 3)         | 1.722 +/- 0.104   | ~1.3   (Zabalo+ 2020)     |
| alpha_eff (log-scaling at p=0.160, Fig 2)    | 0.270 +/- 0.074   | ~1.6   (Li+ 2019)         |
| alpha_vol (volume-law coeff, p=0)            | 0.995 +/- 0.017   | ~1     (system dependent) |
| S_inf (area-law saturation, L=24, p=0.5)     | 0.105 +/- 0.022   | O(1)   (area law)         |
+----------------------------------------------+-------------------+---------------------------+

NOTE: alpha_eff is the coefficient in S(L/2) = alpha_eff * ln(L/2) + const at p_c.
Li et al. (2019) PRB 100, 134306 report alpha(p_c) ~ 1.6 for random Clifford circuits at L >> 100.
Do NOT convert to c_eff = 6*alpha_eff: the MIPT is not a standard CFT.
(Jian, You, Vasseur, Ludwig, PRB 101, 104302, 2020)
